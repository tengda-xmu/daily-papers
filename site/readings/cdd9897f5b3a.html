Comparative Study of Machine Learning Algorithms for Fault Diagnosis in PV Systems Specifically Under Low Irradiance Conditions

Norio Ishikura, Kaima Yamada, Toshiyuki Hamada, Ikuo Nanno, Shinichiro Oke, Masayuki Fujii
EU PVSEC

公开摘要

Photovoltaic (PV) systems continue to expand worldwide, and reliable fault diagnosis has become increasingly important for ensuring safe and stable operation. Although machine-learning-based fault diagnosis has been widely investigated, most previous studies have focused on high-irradiance environments. In contrast, the effectiveness of fault diagnosis under low-irradiance conditions remains insufficiently explored. This study investigates the applicability of machine learning algorithms for diagnosing partial-shading faults in PV systems operating under low-irradiance conditions. An outdoor experimental PV system was used to simulate fault conditions, and current-voltage (I-V) characteristics were collected under various operating conditions. Prior to model development, outlier detection and data preprocessing were performed to improve data quality. Several supervised machine learning algorithms were trained and evaluated using commonly adopted classification metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. The results indicate that fault diagnosis becomes more challenging under low-irradiance conditions because the differences between normal and faulty operating states become less distinct. Consequently, the performance of all evaluated algorithms tended to decrease compared with that observed under conventional irradiance conditions. Nevertheless, reliable fault detection remained feasible. Among the investigated methods, ensemble learning approaches demonstrated superior and more robust performance than conventional classification algorithms. These findings confirm the feasibility of machine-learning-based fault diagnosis for PV systems operating under low-irradiance conditions and highlight the potential of ensemble learning techniques for practical fault detection in regions with limited solar irradiance.
