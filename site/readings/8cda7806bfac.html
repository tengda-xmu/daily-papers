金属增材制造能够实现轻量化和复杂结构，但缺陷、组织各向异性及表面状态造成的疲劳性能波动限制了承载应用。本文摘要介绍了一项遵循PRISMA的系统综述，梳理传统机器学习、深度学习及物理信息神经网络在疲劳预测中的应用，并按缺陷、微观组织和混合信息输入分类。其阅读价值在于整理建模路线、数据稀缺应对策略与研究空白，而非展示某个模型的性能优势。当前仅有摘要，无法核验检索过程、纳入研究质量及定量比较。[摘要]

研究问题

摘要指出，承载应用受到“limited and variable fatigue performance”制约，影响因素包括孔隙、未熔合、组织各向异性、表面条件及后处理。研究问题是如何梳理人工智能对这些复杂因素与疲劳响应关系的建模，而非仅预测单一材料的寿命。[摘要]

方法与路线

作者将研究定义为“PRISMA-guided systematic review”，对象是利用人工智能和机器学习预测金属增材制造疲劳的同行评议论文。综述覆盖浅层学习器、深度学习和物理信息网络，并按输入模态分类；摘要未披露数据库、检索式、纳入数量和质量评价细节。[摘要]

创新与比较

摘要列出缺陷敏感、微观组织感知和混合代理模型三类研究。解读：其可辨识贡献是将算法路线与输入信息组织起来，便于比较不同建模思路，而非提出新的预测算法。“hybrid surrogates”体现多类信息整合，但分类的新颖性仍需全文核验。[摘要]

证据与发现

综述识别的主要研究空白包括“dataset standardization”、物理融合、不确定性量化及跨材料泛化，并整理迁移学习、合成数据增强和交叉验证等策略。摘要没有给出误差、样本量或模型排名，因此不能断言某类算法更准确，也不能量化这些策略的效果。[摘要]

Metal additive manufacturing (AM) has revolutionized the design and production landscape for critical sectors, including aerospace, automotive, and energy. It enables the production of lightweight, topology-optimized, and functionally graded components that are impractical to produce through conventional manufacturing methods. However, the widespread adoption of AM parts in load-bearing applications remains constrained by their limited and variable fatigue performance, strongly influenced by process-induced defects (e.g., porosity, lack of fusion), microstructural anisotropy, surface conditions, and post-processing treatments. In recent years, Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) have emerged as powerful tools for modeling complex process–structure–property–performance relationships and predicting fatigue life, strength, and crack growth in metal AM systems. This study presents a comprehensive PRISMA-guided systematic review of peer-reviewed publications that apply AI and ML for fatigue prediction in metallic AM components. The review synthesizes modeling approaches ranging from conventional “shallow” learners, such as support vector machines (SVM), random forests (RF), and XGBoost, to advanced deep learning frameworks, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and physics-informed neural networks (PINNs). We categorize existing studies based on their input data modalities, including defect-sensitive models that utilize XCT or SEM data, microstructure-aware models that leverage crystallographic features, and hybrid surrogates that integrate process, structure, and property information. Furthermore, we highlight strategies to address data scarcity, including transfer learning, synthetic data augmentation, and cross-validation. Key research gaps are identified in the areas of dataset standardization, physics integration, uncertainty quantification, and cross-material generalization. Finally, the review outlines emerging directions for developing physically grounded, interpretable, and generalizable AI frameworks capable of reliable fatigue performance prediction across diverse AM materials and platforms.
