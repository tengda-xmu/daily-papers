Operational Risks and AI Bias in LLM-Based Agentic Systems for a Process Plant: Electric Motor Operations Case Study (Oil & Gas)

J. Akhtar
ADIPEC

公开摘要

The rapid development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has encouraged many industries to explore Agentic AI. Earlier AI tools were used mainly for data analysis and information retrieval. Newer systems can carry out a sequence of related tasks, combine information from several engineering sources and prepare recommendations with relatively limited user input. These capabilities can support document review, procurement quality, maintenance planning, commissioning and operational troubleshooting. However, they also bring risks that are not normally associated with conventional engineering software. Because the output is probabilistic, a recommendation may be influenced by missing technical information, incomplete operating context, biased source material or incorrect interpretation of safety-critical requirements. It may still appear convincing even when its engineering basis is weak. Electric motors are used throughout oil and gas facilities and are essential to the operation of pumps, compressors, fans, cooling systems and other plant equipment. A motor failure can interrupt production and, in some cases, affect the safety or reliability of the wider process. Decisions relating to motor protection, alarms, equipment condition, commissioning, fault diagnosis and maintenance therefore require sound engineering judgement, supported by approved protection settings, operating history, OEM guidance and relevant international standards. This makes electric motors a practical case for examining the use of LLM-based Agentic AI in a safety-critical engineering environment. The paper considers a series of representative motor scenarios covering design review, Factory Acceptance Testing (FAT), commissioning, operational troubleshooting, predictive maintenance, alarm assessment and root cause analysis. The findings show that prediction accuracy and model confidence alone do not establish whether an AI recommendation is safe or technically reliable. That judgement depends on the quality of the available evidence, the completeness of the operating context, compliance with applicable standards and protection philosophy, and verification by a qualified engineer. To manage these risks, the paper introduces the Engineering Trustworthiness Assurance Framework (ETAF). It brings the main checks into one process, starting with the quality of the available engineering information and ending with the approval of the final recommendation. ETAF includes the Engineering AI Assurance Pyramid (EAAP), the Operational AI Risk Propagation Model (OARPM) and the AI Engineering Governance Matrix (AEGM). It also uses three assessment measures: the Engineering Trustworthiness Score (ETS), AI Recommendation Criticality Level (ARCL) and Engineering Confidence Index (ECI). These are proposed as practical, illustrative tools that organisations can adapt when introducing LLM-assisted decision support in safety-critical engineering work. Although the representative scenarios are based on electric motor operations, the proposed framework is applicable to other classes of rotating equipment deployed throughout upstream, midstream, and downstream oil and gas facilities. The paper contributes a practical engineering governance approach that complements existing AI assurance initiatives by emphasizing engineering trustworthiness, protection philosophy, operational accountability, and responsible human oversight during the deployment of Agentic AI systems.
