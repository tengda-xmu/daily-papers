Cohort Divergence Does Not Consistently Outperform Single-Rollout Judging for Early Warning of LLM Agent Failure

Ejiga Peter Ojonugwa Oluwafemi, Daniel Emakporuena, Caleb Kadiri, Md. Mahmudur Rahman

公开摘要

Early warning of failure would let costly episodes of large language model (LLM) agents be stopped early. Across five model families and five domains (3421 usable rollouts), we test a black-box signal: a rollout’s early divergence in judged goal adherence from sibling rollouts of the same task. Under our original judging protocol, cohort divergence predicted failure. A judge swap revealed the judge had received a task identifier instead of the task objective. Re-judged with the correct objective, the cohort signal fell to chance in retail and airline. A single-rollout judge score has the higher point estimate in eight of ten domain-horizon combinations; only contrasts favouring it survive Holm adjustment. Cohort features add 0.02 held-out AUROC, accounted for by the sibling mean and consistent with task-difficulty information; no per-domain gain survives adjustment. No pre-specified failure-onset profile is confirmed, and a perturbation experiment is inconclusive. Both monitors use privileged access to the full objective; restricted to information the agent had seen, most of their signal disappears. Under an enforced false-abort cap, no monitor has a usable operating point on the original score scale; a finer scale restores conservative ones for four of five models. Judge scores were not validated against human ratings. Judge instructions are a first-order threat to validity in agent-monitoring research.
